The SPARS console is only 15 minutes from my home in Coquitlam.  

I have metal cases made here in Vancouver that hold two M460/M470 14"
modules and I build up my own power supply regulator cards including
phantom power.  I currently rack M460 or M470 modules including the
case, power supplies, connectors and reconditioning of the modules for
$495 Cnd a pair.

I have modified the EQ's on M460 and M462 modules to widen the "Q" from
a minimum 2 down to 1.5 thereby emulating the wider EQ curve of Neve and
API of the era.  

I believe that the Ward Beck's are referred to as the Canadian Neve's
because Roger Beck was approached by the CBC in the early 70's to see if
Ward Beck could build a console that would compare to the Neve consoles
of the time utilizing the Neve 1081 modules.  Roger designed the M460
modules.

The M460 utilizes a custom made Hammond input transformer with the same
winding ratio as the St Ives used in the Neves.  The preamp is also
discrete class "A" just like the Neve except Roger used a class A/B
transistor output stage in the preamp garnishing 6db more headroom in
the front end than Neve.

I am in the process of racking some M470 modules, M460La modules and
building a  rack with 2-M461, 2-M462, 2-M464, 2-M467 and 2-M466 modules.
I have a couple of extra M460LA modules arriving late next week and
these are available for racking...they would be $1000 cnd reconditioned
and racked in our custom 19" cases with power supplies and all
connectors.

I can supply edge connectors for the M460/M470 modules for $7.50 each
new.

It is the weight and size of the consoles that leave a lot of folks no
option but to part out the consoles.  It takes a coordinated effort  as
you probably know to move a Ward Beck console that can weigh over 650
lbs.  I also find that the majority of Ward Beck consoles need to be
completely re-configured for Stereo music recording.  I am always
converting T1202 consoles for stereo monitoring and to send the soloed
signal to both the left and right outputs.

I also have some notes on re-calibrating the VU meters for +4dbm as most
consoles were set for the Broadcast operating level of +8dbm.  

I would glady contribute some technical information to your web-site.  
I was an owner and chief tech at Ocean Sound Studios in Vancouver until
1992.  Ocean had 2 Studer 24 tracks and 2-Trident Series 80 consoles.  I
have also serviced Neve consoles including a BCM10 that I still work on
for a client.  The EQ in the Trident Series 80 and the M460/M462 is very
similar except the WBS has variable "Q" and about 10db more headroom.
