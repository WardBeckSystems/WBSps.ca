Richard Whisnant 



Richards Awesome 8 Space rack of (4) M460A Pre-amps, (2) M466 Compressors tied to (2) M467 Noise gates and "Big ole" VU meters.  This rack was assembled by Mr. Dave Thomas, and the modules came from a console in Vancouver.

This rack is appropriately nick named "THE TANK"
 

History on my rack: Dave Thomas wrote me as follows back when we were doing the deal and planning the rack:

"Yes, I know which console these modules came from.  I dissambled this console my self.  The modules came out of a 32x16 Ward Beck console that was in one of the CBC Television Production studio here in Vancouver.  The console had been well maintained throughout its lifetime by the CBC staff.  All the switches have been replaced with gold plated schadows and noisy pots have been replaced with A&B conductive plastic or Bournes conductive plastic.  The frame was much too large to keep. The pots and switches are cleaned on the re-conditioned modules that I sell.

The Ward Beck consoles use Tantalum capacitors in the outputs which don't deteriote at the same rate as electrolytics used by other manufacturers.  However, if we find or suspect defective components they are replaced.

The microphone inputs are XLR but all the M460 direct outputs are 1/4" balanced and the inputs and outputs to the compressors are 1/4" balanced.  [FYI the inputs are switcheable mic/line] I will send you a jpg of the back of the unit.  I used a ADC type 1/4" balanced patchbay for the M460 outputs and the M466/M467 inputs and outputs.  It is very simple to patch from the output of a M460 into a compressor or chain the M466 and M467 together.  The line inputs to the M460 preamps are also on the 1/4" balanced jacks.

 
I have the power cord, power supply connector finished at the power supply and will pick up a 5 pin XLR male and female connector for the rack end tomorrow.  I have the power supply cable just tacked on the power supply rail but this is just to test.  I want you to have a removable cable at both ends and the rack end must be a female connector at the cable end so that there are no power points exposed when the cable is removed.  I put rubber feet on the bottom of the power supply so it won't scratch your floor.  The bottom of the rack is metal is this a problem or will it be sitting on a table until you get the wooden rack built?

 
The VU meters are working and calibrated to +4dBv into a 10k load.  I am just finishing the phantom power modifiction to the 4-M460 modules.  I use the Solo switch to engage the phantom power for each channel and I have to finish the pilot lights for the Vu meters.  I am planning to ship out the rack on Monday and if not it will go Tuesday for sure. All outputs on the M460 modules are balanced.  The EQ's are not noisy and are very smooth sounding.  As with any compressor if lots of compression is used it will tend to pull up the background noise when the signal falls in level. The line input has an impedance of 30,000 ohms.  This would work fine for synths, keyboards or active instruments but may be a bit too low of an

impedance for a Fender Stratocaster or Precision Bass."

The unit weighs about 37 pounds; the power supply, which attaches with a 10' cable, is another 20 pounds.

Interesting story on this unit is that Dave finished racking it up for me and had it ready to send right on 9/11/01. Given the chaos, it went instead a couple days later, but even then, the large package was apparently pulled apart thoroughly at the border and not well repacked after customs and whoever else inspected it. It was damaged in transit as a result, despite its tank-like appearance. Dave got some settlement from UPS and gladly and willingly fixed the damage, even tweaking a few things in the process. I wholeheartedly recommend him for anyone looking to rack WBS components.
