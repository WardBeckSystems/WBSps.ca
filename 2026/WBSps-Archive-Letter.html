The Ward-Beck archive, sorted

Years here are written the way the archive writes them: 1975(8) is the eighth year after Ward-Beck Systems opened in 1967(0).

Hello,

For years the Ward-Beck Systems collection has been a loose pile of scans, manuals, drawings, photos and old website files. It is now one catalogue: every model has its own page, every drawing is named after its title block, and every year from 1967(0) to today has its place on a timeline.

The originals are kept exactly as they came in. Everything you see is a sorted copy, so nothing has been lost or changed.

Ways to look around

  1. The index binder: typed sheets by category, with each module's sub-assemblies, transformers and the consoles it went into.
  2. The timeline: projects, devices and events year by year, with lines from each project to the modules it used.
  3. The wiki: one page per model, job, year, person and transformer, with manuals split into their sections.
  4. The viewer: open any drawing; a module number printed on it links straight to that module's page.

WILMA, the last WBS

WILMA is a Ward-Beck L3262 split console. She started life as a news console in Florida and became the most heavily modified L series console there is.

  • beforeBuilt as a news console, Florida.
  • 2012(45)Project Wilma: re-homed to Toronto, rewired and redrawn by APKaudio.
  • 2014(47)Rebuilt with every modification at Revolution Recording, Studio C, by Anthony P. Kuzub and Joe Dunphy: 32 M470R pre-amps, 16 custom M436L monitor modules. Sarah Harmer, Whitehorse, Great Lake Swimmers and The Sadies recorded on her.
  • 2025(58)A custom WBS meter bridge carrying on the M436L: the last WBS ever.

Do you have one?

Consoles keep going after they leave their first home, and the archive follows them. Fifteen user consoles are in it already, from Dave's T1202 to Wayne's L2042. If a Ward-Beck console, module or manual has passed through your hands, photos and a line about where it has been would add its next chapter.
