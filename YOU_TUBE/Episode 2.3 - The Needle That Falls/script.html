The Needle That Falls

Ooh honey, let's talk about compression... dynamic control so tight, so responsive, it takes your breath away. From the classic M406 to the seductive M466 and M467, we're watching that gorgeous vertical meter dip and fall as Ward-Beck keeps those hot studio peaks under total, thrilling control.

Timeline

  • 1968|1M406 compressor-limiter: schematic B 406-02-001 and package outline C 406-09-001, July 1968. The archive's manual is CFTO-TV's file copy.
  • 1969|2M406B, December 1969 (schematic B 406-02-010, drawn DJG). Ratios 2:1 to 10:1 plus limit, and a de-ess control giving up to 8 dB of extra high-frequency compression.
  • 1971|4M415 compressor amplifier: a mic/line amplifier with 5:1 compression and an optional expander, in its own case.
  • 1972|5Ad: a TV production console for CFTO-TV and CHAN-TV 'with comprehensive equalizer, compressor'. M406B(C) module layout, March 1972.

  • 1979|12M466N insert meter module schematic, February 1979. Maryland L3242A, job 790236, with the M467.

2.3.1 Cold open: the needle that falls

VO Watch that slender needle dip, darling... slow and smooth. That gentle fall tells you the compressor is doing its job, wrapping its arms around the loudest peaks and keeping everything tight, warm, and intimate.

3. M466 compressor-limiter and M467 noise gate and meter, side by side, 1976|933. B-roll (V2, behind the pictures) M466

2.3.2 Why broadcasters squeeze

VO Broadcasters love to squeeze, honey. In the 80s and 90s, if your signal wasn't loud, punchy, and commanding, listeners would spin the dial. Compression kept the voice hot and present without blowing up the transmitter.

5. M406 manual, 1968|1: 'The object of a compressor is to reduce the dynamic range…'34. B-roll (V2, behind the pictures) M467

2.3.3 1968: the M406 at CFTO

VO It all started in 1968 when CFTO asked for a discrete compressor-limiter that could handle live TV. Ward-Beck delivered the M406—a compact solid-state powerhouse with dual FET gain reduction.

7. M406 manual, 1968|1: 'Property of CFTO-TV Eng. Dept. Do not remove'35. B-roll (V2, behind the pictures) M466M

2.3.4 A compressor the size of a fader

VO Look how slim and sexy it is! Designed to fit right beside a standard channel fader, the M406 didn't hog rack space—it sat right under your fingertips on the main console face.

9. Rodger Beck, 'A Unique Compressor Limiter', page 6: the conclusion36. B-roll (V2, behind the pictures) M406B

2.3.5 Threshold, ratio, attack and release

VO Let's dial in the settings, baby. Set your threshold where it starts to bite, choose a silky 2:1 or a firm 5:1 ratio, and let the lightning-fast attack catch every sudden peak before it gets out of hand.

11. M406B technical data, 1969|2: ratios, 25 dB range, attack and recovery37. B-roll (V2, behind the pictures) ADVERTISEMENTS

2.3.6 The first de-esser

VO Nobody likes harsh, piercing sibilance, darling. Ward-Beck added a high-frequency sidechain loop, creating one of broadcasting's earliest discrete de-essers. It softens those sharp 'S' sounds until they sound like a gentle whisper in your ear.

13. M406B operation: the DE-ESS control, 0 to 8 dB38. B-roll (V2, behind the pictures) M467

2.3.7 Gain reduction you can see

VO A real mixer wants to see what's happening. Those classic round VU meters showed output level, but what you really crave is seeing exactly how much gain the module is taking away.

15. US price schedule, 1 May 1975|8: M406B $435, 'Edgewise gain reduction meter for M406B' $5039. B-roll (V2, behind the pictures) ADVERTISEMENTS

2.3.8 1976: the M466

VO In 1976, Ward-Beck unveiled the ultimate seductress: the M466 compressor-limiter. With its distinctive edgewise meter, adjustable release curves, and whisper-quiet VCA action, it became the gold standard of network television.

17. 466-04-002, M466 front panel, redrawn 10/1977|1040. B-roll (V2, behind the pictures) ADVERTISEMENTS

2.3.9 How the M466 turns the gain down

VO Inside the M466, a precision voltage-controlled attenuator caresses the audio path. As the sidechain detects extra energy, it smoothly pulls back the gain without ever introducing distortion or pumping. Pure, velvet control.

19. B 6-106-02-001, compressor control amplifier schematic, July 1976|941. B-roll (V2, behind the pictures) ADVERTISEMENTS

2.3.10 De-essing, the M466 way

VO Engage the de-ess switch on the M466, and listen to the transformation. It zeroes in on frequencies between 4 and 8 kilohertz, taming the harshness and leaving vocals sounding lush, rich, and utterly seductive.

21. M466 circuit description: 'DE-ESS Control'42. B-roll (V2, behind the pictures) M406B · M466

2.3.11 The vertical meter

VO That narrow vertical meter on the front panel is pure visual temptation. Illuminated from behind, the needle drops from 0 dB down to -20 dB in real time, giving you instant visual feedback on every single word.

23. M466M compressor meter module, 1976|943. B-roll (V2, behind the pictures) M466

2.3.12 The gate beside the meter

VO Right beside the compressor is a whisper-quiet noise gate. When talent stops speaking, the gate gently shuts the door on studio noise, air conditioning rumble, and background chatter. Total silence until you're ready to speak again.

25. B 467-01-001, noise gate and meter module functional diagram, Dec 1976|944. B-roll (V2, behind the pictures) M467
